# Help

A few things in the kit work differently on purpose. This page walks you through a round, then explains why.

## A session, screen by screen

### How do I start an interview? {#start}

> Type who you're meeting, pick the role and the round, then press Start interview.

![The start page: your name as colleagues will see it, who you are meeting, when it starts, the role and the round to run, Start interview, and the check that notes save on this laptop](media/start.webp)

In a kit with Invite, type your own name first, as your colleagues will see it. The interview starts now unless you pick a later time under When does it start?, and then the clock in the header counts down to it. Your plan lays out the activities you ticked at clock times from that start and says when it ends. The round fills in a list of activities: tick or untick any of them and change their minutes, and the total counts the intro and wrap-up too. A line under the list says which competencies the plan can give evidence for. During the call, each activity page has a Minutes box, and Off in the sidebar names what you left out. Less time leaves fewer rows in scope, but the bar stays the same, and a blank row out of scope counts neither way. The role sets the level you evaluate at. Under the form, the kit checks that notes save on this laptop, and lists your recent interviews.

### How do I mark what I saw? {#mark}

> Press a row's number, or click it, to mark it seen. Press it again to clear it. Alt with the number marks it not seen.

![Look for on a question, evaluating at Mid: the interviewer marks two of the three numbered Mid rows seen, with the Junior rows below for placement and Senior folded away](media/mark.mp4)

The rows at the level you are evaluating at come first, numbered. The level below sits under them, so you can place a candidate who is under the bar. Higher levels stay folded away, and so do levels well below, each with a count of the rows you marked seen. Evaluating at changes only your own view.

### What are nudges? {#nudges}

> Some findings carry hints, gentlest first. Give one only when the candidate is stuck.

![A finding with its nudges shown, gentlest first: the first one marked as given, and Gave it beside the next](media/nudges.webp)

Press h to show them. Gave it, or Shift with the row's number, records the next one, so a finding seen after it reads as seen after a nudge. Reading a nudge records nothing.

### What is the checklist on the deep dive? {#passions}

> Ask what the candidate specializes in, or what excites them, and tick it. Ticking a topic also picks its pool.

![The deep dive picker: what the candidate specializes in or is excited by, with CSS ticked, which also picks the CSS pool as the main focus](media/passions.webp)

Add another… takes a passion no pool covers. The picks lead your summary.

### How do I decide? {#decide}

> Open Decide, pick one of the role's decisions, the level you would place them at, and a reason.

![Decide: the role's two decisions, each with what it means, none picked, then the level you would place them at and your reason](media/decide.webp)

Each decision shows what it means, and none is picked for you. Colleagues' evidence opens only once you have both decided.

After you record a decision, Get a second opinion opens a packet to paste into an AI assistant you trust. It leaves out names, decisions and levels, and the kit sends it nowhere. Paste a transcript if you have one, and the AI will check each row against it. The transcript stays in this tab only and is never saved. The candidate's name is replaced where it is spelled as you entered it, but pronouns and other people's names are not, so read it before you paste. If the answer changes your read, use Change my decision. New interview reminds you if you decided without copying the packet, and Undo takes you back.

### How do I interview with a colleague? {#together}

> Open Menu, press Invite, and send the link to your colleague directly. When they knock, press Let in.

Your colleague opens the link, types their name and presses Join. A kit opened from a file gives a code instead, which they paste into Join an interview on their setup page. You see "Alex wants to join" with Let in and Not now, and once they are in, the plan, the passions and the opened questions stay in step on both laptops. Marks, notes and decisions stay on each laptop until you have both decided.

The header names each colleague with a dot. Hover or focus a name to see which step they are on and when you last heard from them. Click a name to follow their page. Esc, a click or typing stops it, and scrolling does not. The status line beside the names says what is happening, such as "Looking for Alex…" or "Lost Alex, reconnecting…", with one next step when something needs you. What you record while offline is saved and catches up.

Change the link in Menu shuts out anyone who still has the old link, and keeps the colleagues you tick. Test connection, on the setup page and in Menu, checks the meeting point before the interview. Sync is experimental, and a kit built without it has no Invite.

## How a round works

A round runs top to bottom in the sidebar. Here's the whole hour in seven steps:

1. **Type the candidate's name.** Start interview needs it.
2. **Pick the role and the round.** The role sets the level you evaluate at. Once you start, the run of show in the sidebar holds the plan, each part with its minutes.
3. **Run the intro.** Follow the script. It reminds you to explain how the hour runs and to leave time for their questions.
4. **Plan the deep dive.** Tick what the candidate specializes in, set the minutes, then check the topics you want, main focus first. The kit picks questions that fit.
5. **Ask and listen, then mark.** Each question lists its Look for rows by level, the ones at your level first and numbered. Mark a row seen when you hear it. Anything else goes in Notes.
6. **Close with the outro.** That time belongs to the candidate's questions.
7. **Decide and copy.** On Decide, pick your decision and the level you would place them at. Copy summary puts your evidence on your clipboard, a dot for what happened to each row.

## Why the kit works this way

Each answer starts with the short version. The ? icons around the kit show that same text and link back here.

### Why is there no score for me to give? {#no-score}

> You tick what you hear, and the kit never turns the ticks into a score. That keeps words like "weak" or "strong" out of your head while the candidate is still talking.

Rating an answer on the spot makes you judge and listen at the same time. A tick asks a smaller question: did you hear this or not.

That matters for the candidate. Once you've labeled an answer "mid", you hear the rest of the interview through that label. That's anchoring.

<!-- guess: Once you've labeled an answer "mid", you hear the rest of the interview through that label. That's anchoring. -->

A great first answer can also make the next ones sound better than they were. That's the halo effect. A tick records what you heard in this answer and nothing more.

<!-- guess: A great first answer can also make the next ones sound better than they were. That's the halo effect. -->

It matters for you too. The first version of the kit asked you to weigh a rubric of 1, 3 and 5 anchors mid-answer. It was too much to read while listening, so it went.

Instead, the summary shows each row with a dot for what happened: ● seen, ◐ seen after a nudge, ○ not seen, · not marked, – not asked. There are no totals or ratios, and a row nobody asked about never reads as a failure.

### Why does the kit never add up my marks? {#score-after}

> A running total would nudge what you ask next. So the kit adds nothing up: it shows each row's mark and leaves the weighing to you, at Decide.

The question card and the sidebar show only the rows you marked. Decide and Copy summary show each level's rows as dots, with no totals. By the time you decide, the candidate has had every chance to show what they know.

Even the little pop when you tick is the same for every item. It confirms the tick and says nothing about how the answer is going.

### Why does the checklist stay short? {#three-items}

> Only the rows at your level are on the checklist, so it stays short enough to read mid-answer, and each number key marks one row.

A pack writes a few rows per level, usually two or three. The level below sits under them for placement, and higher levels stay folded away, so a long ladder never turns into a long list.

The scale is coarse on purpose. Nuance goes in your notes.

You won't find a "needed a follow-up" box either. Whether you asked a follow-up says more about you than about the candidate.

### Why don't the buttons ever move? {#controls-stay-put}

> You're listening while you click. If the buttons shifted with each question's length, you'd hunt for them mid-answer.

The pager stays at the top, and its labels never change width. A long pool name shortens instead of pushing Next down or off the line. Each panel keeps its own spot. A long question scrolls in its own column, so it pushes nothing down.

After one question, your hand knows where Next is. Your eyes can stay on the person.

### How does the kit choose questions? {#pick}

> Check topics in order of focus. The first one gets most of the time, and the kit fills the minutes with questions that fit.

Your first topic is the main focus and gets 60% of the deep dive. The other topics split the rest. Each topic takes its core questions first, then its stretch ones, in the order the author wrote them. Any minutes left over go to whatever still fits.

You never add up minutes during setup, and the picks never run past the deep dive minutes you set. When a topic says "no room", its hint tells you how many minutes it needs.

### Why won't it pick two questions that overlap? {#overlaps}

> Two questions that cover the same ground would spend the candidate's time proving one thing twice.

<!-- guess: Two questions that cover the same ground would spend the candidate's time proving one thing twice. -->

Some questions are marked as overlapping. The pick never puts two of them in one interview. If you open the one it left out, a note says which picked question it overlaps.

For the candidate, every question is a fresh chance to show something new. For you, every answer adds to the picture.

<!-- guess: For the candidate, every question is a fresh chance to show something new. -->

### Why is there a time meter? {#time-meter}

> One glance tells you how full the hour is, so you don't do math while someone talks.

Under the run of show, the total adds up each part's minutes against the minutes the round's author planned. The meter fills as the plan grows and changes color once it runs past. You're allowed to go over. The kit only warns you.

The browser tab shows the time since the interview started. If you set a start time ahead on the setup page, it counts down to it first, such as −4:12. You can see it in the tab strip even while the call fills your screen.

Keeping time is a kindness to the candidate. It protects the outro, which is their turn to ask you questions.

### Why does the kit ask for the candidate's name first? {#name-first}

> The name heads your summary, so your notes never get filed under the wrong person.

<!-- guess: The name heads your summary, so your notes never get filed under the wrong person. -->

You can't start until you type a name. It shows in the header for the whole interview, and Copy summary starts with it. New interview starts a fresh session, so the last person's name never carries over.

It's also a quiet reminder of who you're talking to.

<!-- guess: It's also a quiet reminder of who you're talking to. -->

### What are Notes and Mark done for? {#notes-done}

> Ticks catch what you came to look for. Notes catch everything else, and Mark done shows where you are in the plan.

<!-- guess: Notes catch everything else, and Mark done shows where you are in the plan. -->

A few ticks can't hold a clever fix or an honest "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out." Notes can. They save as you type and show up in Copy summary under their question.

Mark done mutes the question in the run of show. A glance shows what's left. Done with a stretch prompt still to ask says so, and lets you go on.

### What does Copy summary copy? {#copy-summary}

> Plain text for your own notes: the candidate's name, the role and round, what they said they specialize in, each level's rows with a dot for what happened, and your notes.

Copy summary is on Decide. Paste it into whatever doc you keep. It holds your evidence, not the decision you recorded. The kit never tries to read a summary back in. Plain text reads anywhere, and there's no format to break.

### Why is there a Pop out button? {#pop-out}

> It floats the kit in a small window over a fullscreen call, so you can stay with the candidate.

<!-- guess: It floats the kit in a small window over a fullscreen call, so you can stay with the candidate. -->

Pop out moves the whole kit into a small window that stays on top. You can tick and take notes right there, or move to another question. The elapsed time sits in its corner. Close the window and the kit goes back to its tab with nothing lost.

Pop out works in Chrome, Edge and Firefox on desktop. Other browsers don't show the button.

### Why do some questions say Being reviewed? {#needs-check}

> Someone changed the wording, and the content owner hasn't signed off yet. You can still ask it.

The badge only marks the question. The details of what changed stay with whoever keeps the questions. If something reads wrong to you, tell them.

<!-- guess: The details of what changed stay with whoever keeps the questions. If something reads wrong to you, tell them. -->

### Where do my notes go? {#your-data}

> Nowhere. The kit is one file that runs in your browser, and what you type stays in that browser.

The whole kit is a single HTML file. It opens straight from disk and works with no network. The normal kit has no code that sends anything anywhere.

Your interviews are saved in your browser, so a reload loses nothing. A few things to know:

- A private window forgets everything when you close it. Before the interview, on the start page, says whether notes save.
- In Chrome, every page opened from a file on your disk shares that storage. Another local HTML file could read your notes.
- New interview starts a new session and keeps the last one under Recent interviews, with Undo on the start page.
- A decided interview is deleted from this laptop 30 days after its last change, unless your pack sets another number. Five days before, Recent interviews warns you and offers Keep. An interview with no decision stays until you delete it.
- Open the same interview in a second window and it is read-only, with Take over. Close the first window and the second one takes over by itself.

Interview notes are about a real person. Keeping them off anyone's server means there's nothing to leak.

To compare notes with a colleague, Share my evidence saves yours as a file to send them directly. Your marks, notes and decision go in it only after you decide, and their copy stays hidden from them until they decide too. Invite, in Menu, connects colleagues in the same interview the same way: the plan is shared live, and everyone's evidence opens once both of you have decided. Everything crosses the meeting point sealed, so the relay sees only sizes and timing, and keeps nothing.

### How does the kit lighten my load? {#your-load}

> During a call your head is full. The kit tries to leave you only listening and ticking.

<!-- guess: During a call your head is full. The kit tries to leave you only listening and ticking. -->

Here's what it takes off your plate:

- A tick is yes or no. That's faster than weighing a scale.
- One question fills the screen at a time.
- The controls stay put, so your hand knows where Next is.
- The kit does the time math when it picks questions.
- Keyboard shortcuts work on question pages. Press ? to see them.
- The meter and the tab title answer "how are we doing on time?" at a glance.

### Why does the kit barely move? {#motion}

> Motion should help you follow a change, not pull your eyes off the call. If your system asks for reduced motion, nothing moves at all.

<!-- guess: Motion should help you follow a change, not pull your eyes off the call. -->

The few animations are short, like the slide between questions and the pop on a tick. With reduced motion on, they're all off. Clips on the What's new page wait for you to press play.

### What does the kit do for keyboard and screen reader users? {#accessibility}

> Everything works from the keyboard with a clear focus ring, and the colors pass contrast checks in light and dark.

The details:

- Skip to content jumps past the sidebar.
- Every control shows the same focus ring.
- Toggle buttons say whether they're pressed.
- Colors come from your browser and system, or from your pack's own theme when it has one, and text is checked for 4.5:1 contrast. Menu's Colors picks Light, Dark or Auto either way.
- A larger default font switches to the narrower layout sooner, so text never gets cramped.
- All questions searches from the keyboard: Up and Down move through the matches, Enter opens one, Escape clears the search and then closes it, and Tab stays inside until you close it.
- The main screens get an automated accessibility scan on every change.
