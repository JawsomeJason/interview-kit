# Help

A few things in the kit work differently on purpose. This page walks you through a round, then explains why.

## How a round works

A round runs top to bottom in the sidebar. Here's the whole hour in seven steps:

1. **Type the candidate's name.** The round buttons turn on once it's in.
2. **Pick a round.** The sidebar becomes your plan for the hour: the intro, the round, the deep dive and the outro, with the minutes for each.
3. **Run the intro.** Follow the script. It reminds you to explain how the hour runs and to leave time for their questions.
4. **Plan the deep dive.** Set the minutes, then check the topics you want, main focus first. The kit picks questions that fit.
5. **Ask and listen, then tick.** Each question has three Look for items. Tick one when you hear it. Anything else goes in Notes.
6. **Close with the outro.** That time belongs to the candidate's questions.
7. **Review and copy.** Back on the Deep dive page, each question now shows a score. Copy summary puts the whole interview on your clipboard.

## Why the kit works this way

Each answer starts with the short version. The ? icons around the kit show that same text and link back here.

### Why is there no score for me to give? {#no-score}

> You tick what you hear, and the kit works out the score. That keeps words like "weak" or "strong" out of your head while the candidate is still talking.

![On a question, the interviewer ticks two of three Look for items and no score appears. Opening the Deep dive list then shows the question scored Solid, worked out from the ticks](media/ticks-score.mp4)

Rating an answer on the spot makes you judge and listen at the same time. A tick asks a smaller question: did you hear this or not.

That matters for the candidate. Once you've labeled an answer "mid", you hear the rest of the interview through that label. That's anchoring.

A great first answer can also make the next ones sound better than they were. That's the halo effect. A tick records what you heard in this answer and nothing more.

<!-- guess: A great first answer can also make the next ones sound better than they were. That's the halo effect. -->

It matters for you too. The first version of the kit asked you to weigh a rubric of 1, 3 and 5 anchors mid-answer. It was too much to read while listening, so it went.

The score is a count of your ticks:

| Ticks | Score |
| --- | --- |
| None | No score |
| 1 of 3 | Weak |
| 2 of 3 | Solid |
| 3 of 3 | Strong |

### Why does the score only show up after the interview? {#score-after}

> A score next to the question would nudge what you ask next. So it waits on the Deep dive page until you're done.

![The Deep dive list after the interview, where each question you ticked shows Weak, Solid or Strong, above a note that scores come from your Look for ticks](media/score-after.webp)

The question card and the sidebar never show a score. You'll find it in the Deep dive list and in Copy summary. By the time you look, the candidate has had every chance to show what they know.

Even the little pop when you tick is the same for every item. It confirms the tick and says nothing about how the answer is going.

### Why exactly three Look for items? {#three-items}

> Three items put every question on the same scale, so "Solid" means the same thing on every card.

Fewer items made scores uneven. With two items, a single tick already read as Solid. Four or more items brought back the overload of reading a rubric mid-answer.

The scale is coarse on purpose. Nuance goes in your notes.

You won't find a "needed a follow-up" box either. Whether you asked a follow-up says more about you than about the candidate.

### Why don't the buttons ever move? {#controls-stay-put}

> You're listening while you click. If the buttons shifted with each question's length, you'd hunt for them mid-answer.

![Clicking Next through a short question, a long one with code and a medium one, the pager, Notes and the Look for checkboxes stay in the same place while the question text changes length](media/stable-controls.mp4)

The pager stays at the top, and its labels never change width. Each panel keeps its own spot. A long question scrolls in its own column, so it pushes nothing down. Undo floats as a toast for the same reason.

After one question, your hand knows where Next is. Your eyes can stay on the person.

### How does the kit choose questions? {#pick}

> Check topics in order of focus. The first one gets most of the time, and the kit fills the minutes with questions that fit.

![The Deep dive planner at 20 minutes, with CSS checked as the main focus and HTML second, and the four questions the kit picked to fill the time](media/pick.webp)

Your first topic is the main focus and gets 60% of the deep dive. The other topics split the rest. Each topic takes its core questions first, then its stretch ones, in the order the author wrote them. Any minutes left over go to whatever still fits.

You never add up minutes during setup, and the picks never run past the deep dive minutes you set. When a topic says "no room", its hint tells you how many minutes it needs.

### Why won't it pick two questions that overlap? {#overlaps}

> Two questions that cover the same ground would spend the candidate's time proving one thing twice.

### Why is there a time meter? {#time-meter}

> One glance tells you how full the hour is, so you don't do math while someone talks.

![The sidebar timeline, with a meter under the 45 of 45 min total](media/time-meter.webp)

The total in the sidebar adds up the intro, the round, the deep dive and the outro. The meter fills as the plan grows and changes color once it runs past the hour. You're allowed to go over. The kit only warns you.

The browser tab shows the time since the interview started. If you set a start time ahead on the setup page, it counts down to it first, such as −4:12. You can see it in the tab strip even while the call fills your screen.

Keeping time is a kindness to the candidate. It protects the outro, which is their turn to ask you questions.

### What are Notes and Mark done for? {#notes-done}

> Ticks catch what you came to look for. Notes catch everything else, and Mark done shows where you are in the plan.

<!-- guess: Notes catch everything else, and Mark done shows where you are in the plan. -->

![The Notes box holding a line about the answer, the Mark done button showing Done, and two of three Look for items ticked](media/notes-done.webp)

Three ticks can't hold a clever fix or an honest "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out." Notes can. They save as you type and show up in Copy summary under their question.

Mark done mutes the question in the sidebar and the Deep dive list. A glance shows what's left.

### What does Copy summary copy? {#copy-summary}

> Plain text for your own notes: the candidate's name, the round, each question with its ticks and score, and your notes.

Paste it into whatever doc you keep. The kit never tries to read a summary back in. Plain text reads anywhere, and there's no format to break.

The last line names the setup you used, such as the layout and the sidebar. A few of those are still on trial. That line lets the final pick rest on real interviews instead of a guess.

### Why is there a Pop out button? {#pop-out}

> It floats the kit in a small window over a fullscreen call, so you can stay with the candidate.

<!-- guess: It floats the kit in a small window over a fullscreen call, so you can stay with the candidate. -->

![The kit pops out of its tab into a small window over a fullscreen call, where the interviewer ticks a Look for item and types a note while the elapsed time runs, then closes it and the kit returns to the tab](media/pop-out.mp4)

Pop out moves the whole kit into a small window that stays on top. You can tick and take notes right there, or move to another question. The elapsed time sits in its corner. Close the window and the kit goes back to its tab with nothing lost.

Pop out works in Chrome, Edge and Firefox on desktop. Other browsers don't show the button.

![A question with a quiet Being reviewed badge above its title](media/needs-check.webp)

The badge only marks the question. The details of what changed stay with whoever keeps the questions. If something reads wrong to you, tell them.

### Where do my notes go? {#your-data}

> Nowhere. The kit is one file that runs in your browser, and what you type stays in that browser.

The whole kit is a single HTML file. It opens straight from disk and works with no network. The normal kit has no code that sends anything anywhere.

Your round, ticks and notes are saved in your browser, so a reload loses nothing. A few things to know:

- A private window forgets everything when you close it.
- In Chrome, every page opened from a file on your disk shares that storage. Another local HTML file could read your notes.
- New interview clears the last interview and takes you back to the setup page with the same round picked. You get ten seconds to undo it.

Interview notes are about a real person. Keeping them off anyone's server means there's nothing to leak.

An experimental build can share ticks live with a second interviewer. It connects the two laptops directly and never sends notes. The normal kit doesn't include it.

### How does the kit lighten my load? {#your-load}

> During a call your head is full. The kit tries to leave you only listening and ticking.

<!-- guess: During a call your head is full. The kit tries to leave you only listening and ticking. -->

Here's what it takes off your plate:

- A tick is yes or no. That's faster than weighing a scale.
- One question fills the screen at a time.
- The controls stay put, so your hand knows where Next is.
- The kit does the time math when it picks questions.
- Keyboard shortcuts work on question pages. Press ? to see them.
- The meter and the tab title answer "how are we doing on time?" at a glance.

### Why does the kit barely move? {#motion}

> Motion should help you follow a change, not pull your eyes off the call. If your system asks for reduced motion, nothing moves at all.

<!-- guess: Motion should help you follow a change, not pull your eyes off the call. -->

The few animations are short, like the slide between questions and the pop on a tick. With reduced motion on, they're all off. Clips on the What's new page wait for you to press play.

### What does the kit do for keyboard and screen reader users? {#accessibility}

> Everything works from the keyboard with a clear focus ring, and the colors pass contrast checks in light and dark.

The details:

- Skip to content jumps past the sidebar.
- Every control shows the same focus ring.
- Toggle buttons say whether they're pressed.
- Colors come from your browser and system, and text is checked for 4.5:1 contrast.
- A larger default font switches to the narrower layout sooner, so text never gets cramped.
- The main screens get an automated accessibility scan on every change.
